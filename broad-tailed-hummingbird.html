Broad-tailed Hummingbird Behavior

Selasphorus platycercus is one of the few long distance migrant species of hummingbirds, traveling thousands of miles from Mexico to breed in the Rocky Mountains every summer. Males form breeding territories, where they defend willow and wildflowers from intruding males and, most importantly, perform incredible courtship displays for females. Since 2024, I have been studying these behaviors at the Rocky Mountain Biological Laboratory in Colorado to better understand the drivers of individual variation in defense and courtship display effort. This has involved conducting detailed characterization and mapping of individual territories, acoustic recording and playback experiments, and, most recently, radio tracking.
